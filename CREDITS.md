Curieosity is free, and always will be. Its code is licensed **MIT or Apache-2.0**; its

## Text and data

| Source | What the game uses | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/) | Element overviews and many substance descriptions are taken from Wikipedia articles; each page links its article, whose history lists its authors. | CC BY-SA 4.0 |
| [PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) | Names, formulas, properties and synonyms of the compounds (NCBI, U.S. National Library of Medicine), with GHS hazard statements as notified to the ECHA C&L Inventory and use categories from the U.S. EPA's Chemical Data Reporting. | Public domain (U.S. government) |
| [CAMEO Chemicals](https://cameochemicals.noaa.gov/) | Short descriptions of what chemicals look like, from NOAA, with appearance notes from the U.S. National Toxicology Program and Coast Guard. | Public domain (U.S. government) |
| [ChEBI](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/) | Definitions of some compounds, from Chemical Entities of Biological Interest (EMBL-EBI). | CC BY 4.0 |
| [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/) | Identifiers and links that tie the substances to their articles and records. | CC0 |
| [Chemical Reaction Database (CRD)](https://kmt.vander-lingen.nl/) | The library of reactions from patents and the literature, compiled by Rik van der Lingen from Daniel Lowe's reactions extracted from U.S. patents (CC0) and from academic papers. | Open dataset (figshare) |

Every substance page in the game says where its text comes from. Text whose terms didn't
allow it to be shipped was removed from the library by `tools/data/audit_library.py`, and
the substances in play that lost theirs have pages written for the game
(`tools/data/authored_pages.py`).

the reactions and pages are written in `tools/data/`.
